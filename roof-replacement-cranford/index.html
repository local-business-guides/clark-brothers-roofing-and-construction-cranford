What a replacement plan should explain

Replacing a roof generally involves addressing the existing roofing and installing a new system. The exact scope depends on the property and what is found as the work is planned. Ask what removal and preparation are included, which materials and components are specified, and how any underlying issues would be discussed if they come up.

Before you agree, check that you understand:
- Which parts of the existing roof are being removed or retained.
- The proposed roofing materials and related components.
- How disposal and cleanup are covered.
- Whether the proposal addresses flashing, ventilation, and any needed repairs.
- The expected schedule, payment terms, and process for approving changes.

One common mistake is treating every proposal as if it includes the same work. Read the details, compare the scope, and ask about exclusions before choosing. Another is waiting until work is underway to mention access concerns or related exterior plans. Bring those up early and keep the final agreement in writing.

A replacement plan for a home in Cranford should reflect the actual roof and the homeowner's priorities, rather than assumptions about every house in town. Give yourself time to review the proposal before scheduling. Write down remaining questions about materials, preparation, cleanup, or budget, then get answers before approving the work. This keeps your decision grounded in the needs of your Cranford home and the work described on paper.
